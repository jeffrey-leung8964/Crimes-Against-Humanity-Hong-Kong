# Report on Crimes Against Humanity Committed by Hong Kong Government Officials in Hong Kong and Several Other Places

The post-Second World War case United States of America v. Josef Altstötter et al., commonly known as the [Judges’ Trial](https://nuremberg.law.harvard.edu/transcripts/3-transcript-for-nmt-3-justice-case) at Nuremberg confronted the use of legal institutions to perpetrate international crimes. Decades later, [U.S. Senate Resolution 444](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/444) condemned Xi Jinping for orchestrating crimes against humanity and highlighted the repression of fundamental freedoms in Hong Kong. Together, they frame this research’s central question: when does institutionalised repression give rise to individual criminal responsibility?

This independent research, begun in 2025 by an author who subsequently enrolled at the University of California, Berkeley, relies entirely on publicly available materials. The [Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations](https://www.ohchr.org/en/publications/policy-and-methodological-publications/berkeley-protocol-digital-open-source) provides a relevant methodological reference for the collection, verification, and analysis of such materials. Human rights defenders around the world are encouraged to consult the Protocol when documenting abuses and pursuing accountability in their own contexts.

This research argues that the Hong Kong government’s conduct since 2019 constitutes a widespread or systematic attack against a civilian population, amounting to crimes against humanity under international criminal law. 

The research examines political imprisonment, persecution, torture, and transnational repression, connecting documented conduct with the potential individual liability of officials across executive, policing, prosecutorial, judicial, and correctional institutions.

The report’s jurisdictional analysis focuses on the International Criminal Court: although China is not a party to the Rome Statute, it examines whether qualifying nationality or territorial connections could nevertheless enable the Court to exercise jurisdiction over individual Hong Kong officials.

Separately, accountability may also be pursued through national courts exercising universal jurisdiction. Although not examined in this report, torture falls within the treaty-based jurisdictional framework of the UN Convention against Torture (CAT), particularly Articles 5(2) and 7: where an alleged offender is present in territory under a State Party’s jurisdiction, that State must establish jurisdiction and, if it does not extradite the person, submit the case to its competent authorities for the purpose of prosecution.

This repository contains the factual summary, individual liability analysis, evidence bundle, and translated materials, making the research’s sources and reasoning available for scrutiny, further investigation, and accountability efforts.

# 關於香港政府官員在香港及其他若干地方犯下反人類罪的報告

第二次世界大戰後，紐倫堡的「美利堅合眾國訴約瑟夫・阿爾茨托特等人案」（United States of America v. Josef Altstötter et al.），通稱「[法官審判（Judges’ Trial）](https://nuremberg.law.harvard.edu/transcripts/3-transcript-for-nmt-3-justice-case)，直面了利用法律制度實施國際罪行的問題。數十年後，美國參議院[第444號決議](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/444)譴責習近平策劃反人類罪，並指出香港基本自由遭受壓制的情況。兩者共同引出本研究的核心問題：制度化的鎮壓在何種情況下會產生個人刑事責任？

本獨立研究始於2025年，作者其後入讀加州大學柏克萊分校，研究完全以公開可得的資料為依據。《伯克利數字開源調查規程》為此類資料的蒐集、核實及分析提供相關的方法論參考。我們鼓勵世界各地的人權捍衛者在記錄侵害行為及推動問責時，參考該規程並結合各自所處的情境加以運用。

本研究主張，香港政府自2019年以來的行為構成針對平民人口的廣泛或有系統的攻擊，達到國際刑法下反人類罪的構成標準。

本研究分析政治監禁、迫害、酷刑及跨國鎮壓，並將有資料記錄的行為與行政、警務、檢控、司法及懲教機構官員可能承擔的個人刑事責任聯繫起來。

本報告的管轄權分析聚焦於國際刑事法院：儘管中國並非《羅馬規約》締約國，報告探討符合要求的國籍或屬地連結，是否仍可使法院對香港官員個人行使管轄權。

此外，亦可能透過行使普遍管轄權的國家法院追究責任。此途徑未在本報告中探討，但酷刑屬於聯合國《禁止酷刑公約》（CAT）所確立的條約管轄權框架，尤其涉及第5(2)及第7條：當涉嫌犯罪者身處締約國管轄的領土內，該國須確立管轄權，並在不引渡該人的情況下，將案件提交其主管機關，以便進行起訴。

本儲存庫收錄事實摘要、個人責任分析、證據彙編及翻譯資料，公開研究所依據的來源與論證，以供審視、進一步調查及推動問責。
